> [!abstract]
> A pastoral musical interlude about Colette, Colin, and the village fortune-teller, performed before the king in 1752. A highly successful musical-theatrical work, without philosophical content.

Notes

« On disait de J.-J. Rousseau, C'est un hibou. Oui, dit quelqu'un, mais c'est celui de Minerve ; et quand je sors du Devin du village, j'ajouterais, déniché par les Grâces. »

CHAMFORT, Caractères et Anecdotes.

On croira difficilement qu'on ait pu avoir l'idée de faire une parodie du Devin du village ; c'est ce qui a été fait cependant. En septembre 1753 on représenta sous ce titre, à la Comédie-Italienne, les Amours de Bastien et Bastienne, imprimés dans le tome V du Théâtre de Favart (Paris, 1763), et annoncés être l'ouvrage de madame Favart et de M. Harny. Ce n'est autre chose qu'une suite de vaudevilles et airs populaires offrant toutes les scènes et situations de l'opéra-pastorale, sous le travestissement du patois grossier de nos paysans, substitué au langage régulier que Rousseau fait parler à ses personnages. Dans la première scène Bastienne chante, sur l'air, J'ai perdu mon âne,

J'ons perdu mon ami,

D'puis c'temps-là j'n'avons point dormi.

Et deux auteurs ont réuni leurs forces pour cette belle oeuvre ! et cela, dit-on, s'est représenté avec grand succès ! Que penser d'un public qui pouvait alors accueillir de telles pauvretés ? (Note de M. Petitain.)

Ajoutons que, sans faire tort au Devin, la parodie eut un succès mérité. Le public parisien peut être indulgent une fois, ou abusé ; mais il ne persiste pas. Il est vrai que madame Favart contribua beaucoup au succès de la pièce. Mais cette circonstance suffirait pour justifier ce public (comme Valérie sous nos yeux ), s'il n'y avait pas eu d'autres motifs de succès. (Note de M. Musset-Pathay.)

Scène I.

Colette

COLETTE, soupirant, et s'essuyant les yeux de son tablier.

J'ai perdu tout mon bonheur ;

J'ai perdu mon serviteur ;

Colin me délaisse.

Hélas ! il a pu changer !

Je voudrais n'y plus songer :

J'y songe sans cesse.

J'ai perdu mon serviteur ;

J'ai perdu tout mon bonheur ;

Colin me délaisse.

Il m'aimait autrefois, et ce fut mon malheur.

Mais quelle est donc celle qu'il me préfère ?

Elle est donc bien charmante ! Imprudente bergère !

Ne crains-tu point les maux que j'éprouve en ce jour ?

Colin m'a pu changer ; tu peux avoir ton tour.

Que me sert d'y rêver sans cesse ?

Rien ne peut guérir mon amour,

Et tout augmente ma tristesse.

J'ai perdu mon serviteur ;

J'ai perdu tout mon bonheur ;

Colin me délaisse.

Je veux le haïr... je le dois...

Peut-être il m'aime encore... Pourquoi me fuir sans cesse ?

Il me cherchait tant autrefois !

Le Devin du canton fait ici sa demeure ;

Il sait tout ; il saura le sort de mon amour :

Je le vois, et je veux m'éclaircir en ce jour.

Scène II.

Le Devin, Colette

Tandis que le Devin s'avance gravement, Colette compte dans sa main de la monnaie, puis elle la plie dans un papier, et la présente au Devin, après avoir un peu hésité à l'aborder.

COLETTE, d'un air timide.

Perdrai-je Colin sans retour ?

Dites-moi s'il faut que je meure.

LE DEVIN, gravement.

Je lis dans votre [coeur](/positions/fideism), et j'ai lu dans le sien.

COLETTE.

O dieux !

LE DEVIN.

Modérez-vous.

COLETTE.

Eh bien ?

Colin...

LE DEVIN.

Vous est infidèle.

COLETTE.

Je me meurs.

LE DEVIN.

Et pourtant il vous aime toujours.

COLETTE, vivement.

Que dites-vous ?

LE DEVIN.

Plus adroite et moins belle,

La dame de ces lieux...

COLETTE.

Il me quitte pour elle !

LE DEVIN.

Je vous l'ai déjà dit, il vous aime toujours.

COLETTE, tristement.

Et toujours il me fuit !

LE DEVIN.

Comptez sur mon secours.

Je prétends à vos pieds ramener le volage.

Colin veut être brave, il aime à se parer :

Sa vanité vous a fait un outrage

Que son amour doit réparer.

COLETTE.

Si des galants de la ville

J'eusse écouté les discours,

Ah ! qu'il m'eût été facile

De former d'autres amours !

Mise en riche demoiselle,

Je brillerais tous les jours ;

De rubans et de dentelle

Je chargerais mes atours.

Pour l'amour de l'infidèle

J'ai refusé mon bonheur ;

J'aimais mieux être moins belle

Et lui conserver mon coeur.

LE DEVIN.

Je vous rendrai le sien, ce sera mon ouvrage.

Vous, à le mieux garder appliquez tous vos soins ;

Pour vous faire aimer davantage,

Feignez d'aimer un peu moins.

L'amour croît, s'il s'inquiète ;

Il s'endort, s'il est content :

La bergère un peu coquette

Rend le berger plus constant.

COLETTE.

À vos sages leçons Colette s'abandonne.

LE DEVIN.

Avec Colin prenez un autre ton.

COLETTE.

Je feindrai d'imiter l'exemple qu'il me donne.

LE DEVIN.

Ne l'imitez pas tout de bon ;

Mais qu'il ne puisse le connaître.

Mon art m'apprend qu'il va paraître ;

Je vous appellerai quand il en sera temps.

[Arv. ed]

Scène III.

Le Devin

LE DEVIN.

J'ai tout su de Colin, et ces pauvres enfants

Admirent tous les deux la science profonde

Qui me fait deviner tout ce qu'ils m'ont appris.

Leur amour à propos en ce jour me seconde ;

En les rendant heureux, il faut que je confonde

De la dame du lieu les airs et les mépris.

Scène IV.

Le Devin, Colin

COLIN.

L'amour et vos leçons m'ont enfin rendu sage,

Je préfère Colette à des biens superflus :

Je sus lui plaire en [habit](/concepts/custom) de village,

Sous un habit doré qu'obtiendrais-je de plus ?

LE DEVIN.

Colin, il n'est plus temps, et Colette t'oublie.

COLIN.

Elle m'oublie, ô ciel ! Colette a pu changer !

LE DEVIN.

Elle est femme, jeune et jolie ;

Manquerait-elle à se venger ?

COLIN.

Non, Colette n'est point trompeuse,

Elle m'a promis sa foi :

Peut-elle être l'amoureuse

D'un autre berger que moi ?

LE DEVIN.

Ce n'est point un berger qu'elle préfère à toi,

C'est un beau monsieur de la ville.

COLIN.

Qui vous l'a dit ?

LE DEVIN, avec emphase.

Mon art.

COLIN.

Je n'en saurais douter.

Hélas qu'il va m'en coûter

Pour avoir été trop facile !

Aurais-je donc perdu Colette sans retour ?

LE DEVIN.

On sert mal à la fois la fortune et l'amour.

D'être si beau garçon quelquefois il en coûte.

COLIN.

De grâce, apprenez-moi le moyen d'éviter

Le coup affreux que je redoute.

LE DEVIN.

Laisse-moi seul un moment consulter.

(Le Devin tire de sa poche un livre de grimoire et un petit bâton de Jacob, avec lesquels il fait un charme. De jeunes paysannes, qui venaient le consulter, laissent tomber leurs présents, et se sauvent tout effrayées en voyant ses contorsions.)

Le charme est fait. Colette en ce lieu va se rendre.

Il faut ici l'attendre.

COLIN.

A l'apaiser pourrai-je parvenir ?

Hélas ! voudra-t-elle m'entendre ?

LE DEVIN.

Avec un coeur fidèle et tendre

On a droit de tout obtenir.

(à part.)

Sur ce qu'elle doit dire allons la prévenir.

Scène V.

COLIN.

Je vais revoir ma charmante maîtresse.

Adieu, châteaux, grandeurs, richesse,

Votre éclat ne me tente plus.

Si mes pleurs, mes soins assidus,

Peuvent toucher ce que j'adore,

Je vous verrai renaître encore,

Doux moments que j'ai perdus.

Quand on sait aimer et plaire,

A-t-on besoin d'autre bien ?

Rends-moi ton coeur, ma bergère,

Colin t'a rendu le sien.

Mon chalumeau, ma houlette,

Soyez mes seules grandeurs ;

Ma parure est ma Colette,

Mes trésors sont ses faveurs.

Que de seigneurs d'importance

Voudraient bien avoir sa foi !

Malgré toute leur puissance,

Ils sont moins heureux que moi.

Scène VI.

Colin, Colette, parée.

COLIN, à part.

Je l'aperçois... Je tremble en m'offrant à sa vue...

... Sauvons-nous... Je la perds si je fuis...

COLETTE, à part.

Il me voit... Que je suis émue !

Le coeur me bat...

COLIN.

Je ne sais où j'en suis.

COLETTE.

Trop près, sans y songer, je me suis approchée.

COLIN.

Je ne puis m'en dédire, il la faut aborder.

(à Colette d'un ton radouci, et d'un air moitié riant, moitié embarrassé.)

Ma Colette...êtes-vous fâchée ?

Je suis Colin, daignez me regarder.

COLETTE, osant à peine jeter les yeux sur lui.

Colin m'aimait, Colin m'était fidèle :

Je vous regarde, et ne vois plus Colin.

COLIN.

Mon coeur n'a point changé ; mon erreur trop cruelle

Venait d'un sort jeté par quelque esprit malin :

Notes

“People used to say about J.-J. Rousseau that he was a ‘bouwer’ (a kind of owl). ‘Yes,’ someone would reply, ‘but it’s Minerva’s bouwer; and when I come out of the ‘Devin du village’ (Rousseau’s works), I’d add that it was also ‘discovered’ by the Grâces.’”

CHAMFORT, Characters and Anecdotes.

It would be hard to believe that anyone could have thought of creating a parody of “The Village Deacon”; yet precisely that was done. In September 1753, at the Comédie-Italienne, “The Loves of Bastien and Bastienne” was performed under this title. This work was included in Volume V of “The Theatre of Favart” (Paris, 1763) and was advertised as being the collaboration of Madame Favart and Monsieur Harny. In reality, it consisted merely of a series of vaudevilles and popular songs that reproduced all the scenes and situations of the pastoral opera, but presented in the rough dialect of our peasants—instead of the refined language Rousseau used for his characters. In the first scene, Bastienne sings to the tune of “I’ve Lost My Donkey.”

I have lost my friend.

Since that time, we have not slept at all.

And two authors joined their efforts to create this beautiful work! And it is said that it was a great success when it was performed! What can one think of an audience that was capable of appreciating such poor-quality performances at that time? (Note by Mr. Petitain.)

It should also be noted that, without being unfair to the author, the parody was indeed a deserved success. The Parisian audience may indulge once, or even be deceived; but it does not persist in such behavior. It is true that Madame Favart played a significant role in the play’s success. However, this alone would be enough to justify the audience’s reaction (just as Valérie does before our eyes), if there were no other factors contributing to its success. (Note by M. Musset-Pathay.)

Scene I.

Colette

“Colette,” she sighed, wiping her eyes with her apron.

I have lost all my happiness;

I have lost my servant;

Colin is leaving me behind.

Alas! It was possible for it to change!

I would like to stop thinking about it altogether:

I think about it constantly.

I have lost my servant;

I have lost all my happiness;

Colin is leaving me behind.

He once loved me, and that was my misfortune.

But which one does he prefer, after all?

She is indeed very charming! What an imprudent shepherdess!

Do you not fear the evils that I am suffering today?

Colin was able to change me; now it’s your turn.

What good is it for me to keep dreaming about it endlessly?

Nothing can heal my love.

And everything only increases my sadness.

I have lost my servant;

I have lost all my happiness;

Colin is leaving me behind.

I want to hate him. I have to.

Perhaps he still loves me. Why does he keep running away from me?

He used to look for me so much back then!

The Prophet of the Canton resides here;

He knows everything; he will know the fate of my love:

I see it, and I wish to gain clarity on this matter today.

Scene II.

“The Seer,” Colette

As the Divine Being stepped forward solemnly, Colette counted some coins in her hand, then wrapped them up in paper and presented them to the Divine Being after hesitating for a moment before approaching him.

“COLETTE,” said she in a timid voice.

Will I lose Colin forever?

Tell me if I must die.

“The prophet,” said he solemnly.

I read within your [-heart](/positions/fideism), and I also read within his.

COLETTE.

Oh gods!

THE SEER.

Moderate your behavior.

COLETTE.

Well?

Colin.

THE SEER.

He is unfaithful to you.

COLETTE.

I am dying.

THE SEER.

And yet, he still loves you.

“COLETTE,” quickly.

What do you say?

THE SEER.

More agile but less beautiful.

The lady of these lands.

COLETTE.

He is leaving me for her!

THE SEER.

I have already told you, he still loves you.

“COLETTE,” said she, sadly.

And yet, he always escapes from me!

THE SEER.

Count on my help.

I claim to bring back what has flown away, at your feet.

Colin wants to be brave; he enjoys dressing up.

His vanity has caused you great offense.

That his love must make amends.

COLETTE.

If some gallant gentlemen from the city.

I would have listened to the speeches.

Ah! How easy it would have been for me.

To nurture other forms of love!

A wealthy young lady.

I would shine every day.

Of ribbons and lace.

I would equip myself with all necessary items.

For the sake of the infidel.

I refused my own happiness;

I would have preferred to be less beautiful.

And to keep my heart for him.

THE SEER.

I will return yours; it will be my own work.

At best, you devote all your care to it;

To make you love me even more,

Pretend to love a little less.

Love grows when it is nourished by concern;

He falls asleep if he is happy.

The somewhat coquettish shepherdess

Make the shepherd more steadfast.

COLETTE.

To your wise teachings, Colette yields herself entirely.

THE SEER.

When it’s Colin, use a different tone.

COLETTE.

I would be reluctant to imitate the example he sets for me.

THE SEER.

Don’t really imitate him;

But that he may not come to know it.

My art teaches me that it will appear.

I will call you when the time comes.

Scene III.

The Seer

THE SEER.

I knew everything about Colin, and those poor children.

Both of them admired that profound science.

Which makes me able to deduce everything they have taught me.

Their love on this day lends me strength;

In order to make them happy, I must confuse things for them.

The airs of disdain and contempt from the local lady.

Scene IV.

The seer, Colin.

COLIN.

Love and your teachings have finally made me wise.

I prefer Colette to unnecessary possessions.

I knew I could please her by wearing a [village dress](/concepts/custom).

Under a golden garment, what else could I gain?

THE SEER.

Colin, there is no more time left, and Colette has forgotten about you.

COLIN.

She has forgotten me, oh heavens! Colette has been able to change!

THE SEER.

She is a woman, young, and beautiful.

Would she fail to take her revenge?

COLIN.

No, Colette is not deceitful.

She promised me her faith:

Could she be his lover?

By another shepherd than me?

THE SEER.

It is not a shepherd that she prefers to you.

He is a fine gentleman from the city.

COLIN.

Who told you that?

“The prophet,” he said with emphasis.

My art.

COLIN.

I would have no doubt about it.

Alas, what a price it will cost me.

For being too easy!

Had I then lost Colette forever?

THE SEER.

It is impossible to serve both wealth and love well at the same time.

Being such a handsome boy sometimes comes at a price.

COLIN.

Please, teach me how to avoid it.

That terrible blow which I fear.

THE SEER.

Let me be alone for a moment to think it over.

(The sorcerer takes out a grimoire and a small Jacob’s staff from his pocket, and uses them to cast a spell. Young peasant women who came to consult him drop their gifts and flee in terror at seeing his grotesque movements.)

The charm is now in place; Colette will go to that place.

One must wait for it here.

COLIN.

Could I manage to calm him down?

Alas! Will she even want to hear me?

THE SEER.

With a faithful and tender heart

One has the right to obtain anything.

(in a low voice.)

Let’s go warn her about what she has to say.

Scene V.

COLIN.

I will see my charming mistress again.

Farewell, castles, greatness, wealth.

Your brilliance no longer tempts me.

If my tears, my diligent care,

They cannot touch what I adore.

I will see you reborn once more.

Such tender moments that I have lost.

When one knows how to love and please others.

Is there any other good that is needed?

Give me your heart, my shepherdess.

Colin gave you his.

My pipe, my tobacco pipe.

Be my only source of pride;

My attire is my Colette.

My treasures are his favors.

What many important lords there were.

They would truly like to possess her faith!

Despite all their power,

They are less happy than I am.

Scene VI.

Colin, Colette—get ready.

“COLIN,” he said to himself.

I see him. I tremble as I am exposed to his gaze.

. Let’s save ourselves. If I run away, I’ll lose her.

COlette, in a low voice.

He sees me. How moved I am!

My heart is beating.

COLIN.

I don’t know where I am anymore.

COLETTE.

Too close—without realizing it, I had gotten too near.

COLIN.

I cannot deny it; we must address it.

(to Colette, in a tone that was both softened and half-laughing, half-embarrassed.)

My Colette, are you angry?

I am Colin; please look at me.

Colette, barely daring to look at him.

Colin loved me; Colin was faithful to me:

I look at you, but I no longer see Colin.

COLIN.

My heart has not changed; my mistake was too cruel.

It came from a spell cast by some mischievous spirit:

Note

Si diceva di J.-J. Rousseau: “È un gufo”. “Sì”, rispose qualcuno, “ma è il gufo di Minerva; e quando esco dal ‘Devin du village’, aggiungerei che è stato scoperto dalle Grazie”.

CHAMFORT, Caratteri e aneddoti.

È difficile credere che qualcuno possa aver avuto l’idea di fare una parodia del “Devin du village”; eppure è proprio ciò che è stato fatto. Nel settembre 1753, alla Comédie-Italienne, fu rappresentato con questo titolo “Les Amours de Bastien et Bastienne”, pubblicato nel volume V del “Théâtre de Favart” (Parigi, 1763), e presentato come opera di madame Favart e di M. Harny. In realtà si trattava soltanto di una serie di vaudevilles e canzoni popolari che riproducevano tutte le scene e le situazioni dell’opera pastorale, presentate sotto forma del dialetto grossolano dei nostri contadini, al posto del linguaggio normale utilizzato da Rousseau nei suoi personaggi. Nella prima scena, Bastienne canta la canzone “J’ai perdu mon âne”.

Ho perso il mio amico.

Da allora non ho più dormito.

E due autori hanno unito le loro forze per realizzare quest’opera meravigliosa! Si dice che abbia avuto un grande successo. Che cosa si può pensare di un pubblico che all’epoca era disposto ad accettare simili “poverezze artistiche”? (Nota di M. Petitain.)

Aggiungiamo che, senza offendere il “Devin”, la parodia ebbe un successo meritato. Il pubblico parigino può essere indulgente una volta, o anche abusare della sua tolleranza; ma non persiste in tale comportamento. È vero che madame Favart contribuì molto al successo dell’opera. Ma questa circostanza basterebbe a giustificare il favore del pubblico, se non ci fossero stati altri motivi che contribuirono al suo successo. (Nota di M. Musset-Pathay.)
